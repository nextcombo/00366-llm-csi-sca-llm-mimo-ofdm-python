In recent years, the success of large language models (LLMs) has sparked interest in their potential applications in wireless communication, particularly in channel prediction tasks. However, when directly applied to channel prediction, LLMs face the issue of domain mismatch due to their pre-training on textual data. To alleviate this problem, the "adaptor + LLM" paradigm has emerged —— designed adaptors are introduced to bridge the gap between channel state information (CSI) data and LLMs. Despite initial success, existing adaptors have not fully realized their potential.
To address this limitation, this study proposes a key insight: learning representations from spectral components of CSI features can more effectively bridge the domain gap. Based on this, this paper introduces a spectral attention framework named SCA-LLM for channel prediction in multi-input multi-output Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (MIMO-OFDM) systems. Specifically, the novel adaptor in this framework captures finer spectral details compared to previous methods, enabling the LLM to better adapt to channel prediction tasks. Extensive simulation results show that SCA-LLM achieves current state-of-the-art prediction performance and strong generalization capabilities, with a normalized mean squared error (NMSE) advantage of up to -2.4 dB compared to previous methods based on LLMs. Ablation studies further confirm that SCA-LLM significantly outperforms in mitigating domain mismatch issues.
Introduction: Multi-Input Multi-Output Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (MIMO-OFDM) [1] is a key technology in 5G and future communication systems [2,3] that provides reliable and high-speed wireless communication capabilities. This technology combines the spatial advantages of MIMO [4,5] with the ability of OFDM to handle frequency selective fading [6], thereby significantly enhancing the spectral efficiency and link stability of MIMO-OFDM systems [1]. To further optimize performance, modern deployments often employ dynamic resource allocation [7], precoding [8], beamforming [9,10] and other adaptive technologies —— all of which rely heavily on accurate and timely channel state information (CSI) acquisition by both transmitters and receivers.
However, in practical applications, the acquisition of CSI faces numerous challenges: wireless channels rapidly change over time, leading to quickly outdated CSI, a phenomenon known as "channel aging" [11], which is particularly pronounced in scenarios with high round-trip times such as high-speed mobile environments or Non-Terrestrial Networks (NTN). Additionally, frequent channel estimation processes require substantial pilot overhead, consuming valuable communication resources. To address these issues, channel prediction has emerged as a highly promising technology: by predicting future channel conditions, transmission strategies can be actively optimized, thereby mitigating the negative impact of channel aging and significantly reducing the need for frequent pilot transmissions, enabling adaptive MIMO-OFDM systems to fully leverage their performance potential.
A. Relevant research channel prediction methods can generally be divided into traditional methods and deep learning (DL) based methods.
•  
Traditional methods: Typically based on explicit mathematical models derived from statistical hypotheses or simplified channel physical models, but struggle to achieve superior performance in complex dynamic wireless environments. For example, advanced models such as the Prony algorithm-based approach - the Delay Domain (PAD) method [13] - while capable of capturing specific channel structures and enhancing accuracy under certain conditions, have fundamental limitations: despite providing analytical guarantees, PAD requires frequent parameter updates to adapt to environmental changes [14], leading to substantial computational overhead. There is still a need for more robust and generalizable channel prediction methods to effectively adapt to diverse environments.
•
Deep learning-based approaches [14-20]: As a promising alternative, these methods do not rely on predefined mathematical models but learn complex dependencies and predictive patterns directly from historical channel observations through data-driven approaches. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) [16], Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks [18], Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) [19], and Transformers [20] have been utilized to model the complex spatiotemporal dynamics of wireless channels. For instance, in the context of high-speed mobile massive MIMO systems - the delay domain, literature [14] proposes a prediction method based on a Complex Numerical Neural Network (CVNN), which learns directly from historical channel data representations in the delay domain, achieving higher prediction accuracy than traditional PAD methods [13]; literature [20] introduces a channel prediction method based on Transformers, leveraging the attention mechanism within the model to establish correlations between historical CSI and future channels. By focusing on the most relevant historical information, it enhances both prediction accuracy and communication performance, and also proposes a unified pilot-precoder scheme that integrates channel estimation, prediction, and precoding with a single Transformer model, reducing computational complexity.
Although these deep learning-based methods have higher accuracy compared to traditional methods, there are still limitations in practical applications: the models are usually trained for specific tasks and environments. When encountering unseen communication environments without extensive retraining, due to limited sequence modeling capabilities, they often exhibit insufficient robustness. Therefore, it is urgent to develop new methods that are more robust for unknown environments.
In recent years, large language models (LLMs) [21-24] have demonstrated exceptional pattern recognition and sequence modeling capabilities through training on massive amounts of sequence data. Their success has propelled the application of LLMs to research on physical layer problems in wireless communication [25-28]. Channel prediction, as a natural sequence prediction task that aligns well with the strengths of LLMs, has become one of the key research directions. However, when applying predominantly text-based LLMs directly to specialized physical layer signals such as wireless channels, there is a severe issue of domain mismatch: LLMs pretrained on textual data struggle to understand the complex spatiotemporal dynamics dominated by the characteristics of wireless channels. Therefore, their direct application to raw or lightly processed CSI data significantly limits their performance in channel prediction tasks (further ablation studies will validate this point).
To address this issue, recent studies such as LLM4CP [29] propose the "adaptor + large language model" paradigm for channel prediction. By preprocessing CSI data through a dedicated adaptor and then inputting into a pre-trained large language model, LLM4CP employs an adapter inspired by SE-Net [30] that includes a channel attention layer (referred to as the CSI attention module) for feature analysis and modeling. The aim is to capture spatiotemporal correlations in CSI data and transform it into features more easily interpretable for subsequent large language models, thereby mitigating the domain mismatch problem. As a preliminary attempt, LLM4CP has validated the feasibility and potential of the "adaptor + large language model" paradigm.

However, the effectiveness of the LLM4CP adapter relies on the generation of channel attention weights in its CSI attention module through global average pooling (GAP) operations—which only retain the lowest frequency component of the spatiotemporal feature spectrum, discarding all other frequency components [31]. Although experimental results in the literature [29] suggest that this representation learning method focusing solely on the lowest frequency component can still help model multipath effects and extract latent physical propagation features, the discarded spectral components also contain valuable information about the potential characteristics of wireless channels. This information reflects phenomena such as multipath effects in specific scattering environments, temporal-spatial correlations of antenna arrays, Doppler effects caused by mobility, and environmental noise during transmission. Since accurate modeling of MIMO channels depends on these details, discarding this information limits the performance potential of LLM4CP. Therefore, despite these advances, existing methods still significantly lag behind in fully leveraging large language models for channel prediction.

Research Contributions To bridge this gap, this study proposes SCA-LLM—a spectral attention framework combined with pretrained large language models on massive general knowledge data, for precise channel prediction in MIMO-OFDM systems. The framework consists of three sequential components: spectral attention adapter, pretrained large language model, and output head. The specific research contributions are as follows:
1. Proposes SCA-LLM, a novel spectral attention framework for MIMO-OFDM channel prediction, effectively mitigating domain mismatch issues by integrating a pretrained large language model with a dedicated spectral attention adapter.
Key insights: Learning representations from the spectral components of CSI features can more effectively bridge the gap between CSI data and large language models. Based on this, a Spectral Channel Adaptation (SCA) module is introduced in the adapter, which employs a multi-spectral channel attention layer and utilizes two-dimensional Discrete Cosine Transform (2-D DCT) [31,32] for feature analysis and modeling. Unlike the CSI attention module of LLM4CP, the SCA module selectively retains multiple spectral components (from low to high frequencies) in the features using multiple DCT basis functions, helping SCA-LLM better model wireless channel characteristics and more effectively bridge the domain gap between CSI data and large language models, thereby significantly improving prediction performance.

Conclusion: This paper proposes the SCA-LLM framework for precise and robust channel prediction in MIMO-OFDM systems. The framework alleviates domain mismatch issues by designing a novel spectral attention adapter, effectively integrating large language models with CSI data. Within this adapter, the SCA module employs a multi-spectral channel attention mechanism that combines two-dimensional DCT, compared to methods relying on global pooling operations, it can capture broader spectral components from CSI features, thus providing a more suitable input for the large language model, significantly enhancing its adaptation capability and predictive performance.
